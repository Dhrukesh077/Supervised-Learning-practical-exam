# Summary Report — House Price Prediction (Ames Housing)

**Business problem and dataset.** A PropTech start-up needs an automated estimator that gives buyers and sellers a fast, objective benchmark price. I framed this as supervised regression on the Ames Housing `train.csv` (1,460 sales, 79 features, target `SalePrice` in USD). The target is right-skewed (skewness 1.88), so I modelled `log1p(SalePrice)` and converted every prediction back with `expm1`; all metrics are reported in USD.

**Preprocessing and feature engineering.** Missing values followed the exam rules: columns with more than 80 % missing (`PoolQC`, `MiscFeature`, `Alley`, `Fence`) were dropped, categorical "absent" values became `None`, absent areas and counts became 0, and `LotFrontage` / `GarageYrBlt` were median-imputed. Two high-leverage, unfinished sales (GrLivArea > 4000 with price < $300k) were removed because they bent the linear fit. I created `TotalSF`, `HouseAge`, `RemodAge`, `HasGarage` and `HasPool`, ordinal-encoded quality columns (Po→1 … Ex→5), one-hot encoded low-cardinality categoricals, mean-target-encoded `Neighborhood` and three other high-cardinality columns, applied `log1p` to skewed area columns and standardised all numerics. Every data-dependent step sits inside one scikit-learn `Pipeline` fitted on the 80 % training split only, so there is no leakage; the saved model accepts raw rows.

**Best model.** Five models plus a tuned XGBoost were compared. **Ridge Regression** was chosen: lowest 5-fold CV RMSE ($22,655 ± $2,742) and lowest test log-RMSE (0.113), with hold-out RMSE **$19,763**, MAE $14,242 and R² **0.929** (about 72 % of test houses within ±10 % of the true price). Lasso ($19,621) and tuned XGBoost ($20,350) were statistically indistinguishable, so I preferred the model that is simplest to explain, trains in milliseconds and is deterministic. Random Forest was weakest (CV RMSE $26,839). Residuals are centred at zero with no trend or funnel on the log scale, but have a heavy left tail: the model over-predicts cheap, low-quality or abnormal sales.

**Top predictive features and what they mean.** (1) **Living area (`GrLivArea`)**: adding 500 sq ft raises the predicted price about 12 %. (2) **Build quality (`OverallQual`)**: each point is worth about 4.5 %, and a house rated 10 is predicted about 25 % above the same house rated 5. (3) **Condition (`OverallCond`)**: about 4 % per point. Basement area and neighbourhood follow; the same house costs about 14 % more in NridgHt than in MeadowV. For a real-estate professional, price is driven by how big, how well built and where; adding usable space and improving build quality give the strongest value uplift.

**Next steps for production.** Add more data and local features (micro-location, school ratings, listing text, time trends); stack Ridge with gradient boosting; use quantile or conformal methods for asymmetric prediction intervals; route low-quality or abnormal-sale listings to human review; monitor drift and retrain regularly; and, with enough data, explore Bayesian hierarchical models by neighbourhood.
